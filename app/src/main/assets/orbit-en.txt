The Quiet Orbit

At four in the morning the station passed over the night side of Earth. The instrument lights drifted across the window, scattering little green stars among the real ones.

Maya closed her logbook. The last transmission had arrived forty minutes earlier, but she could still hear the controller's voice: “If it gets too quiet, look down.”

Between two banks of clouds, a city flickered. From up here its streets looked like a letter nobody had decided how to finish. Maya picked a single light and followed it with her eyes until it slipped past the window frame.

“There you are,” said Eli behind her.

He set two cups beside the window. One was marked Morning. The other said Still Night.

“Which one?”

Maya took the night. There were twenty seconds until sunrise, enough for a sip and the small shiver of the station as the pumps switched on.

A thin blue line appeared above the horizon. Eli didn't say anything. They stood there with their cups until the shadows of the window frames slowly came into view on the opposite wall.
